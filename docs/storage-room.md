# Room for progress in the browser copy

Written at v9.18 (October 2026), when Vocab pack 9 brought the course to 18,747 items that can hold progress, and updated on 3 November 2026 (v9.24, still 18,747), when the progress test's line was raised from 3 MB to 4 MB (option 1 below). Before adding a big pack, check the room figure the progress test prints in section 8; read this before changing how progress is saved.

## Where things stand

- **What the test measures.** `tools/test-progress.js` (section 8) saves a record for every item in the course, each with figures as long as they get (box 7, 123 answers, 12 lapses, four timestamps), plus two years of daily log, and checks that the browser copy (`itapp.progress.v1` in localStorage) stays under the **4 MB line** (`BROWSER_LINE`, at the top of the test). At v9.24 it is **2.95 MB**, and the test prints how many more records fit under the line: about **7,160**, roughly seven 1,000-card vocab packs. When fewer than 2,000 fit (about two packs), it also prints a note that it's time for option 3.
- **The 4 MB is our own safety line, not the browser's limit.** Chromium, which Chrome on Android uses, lets a site keep about 5 MiB of text in localStorage, keys and values together, shared by every key the site saves: 5,242,240 characters when this note was first written, and exactly 5 MiB (5,242,880 characters) in Chromium 153, measured in November 2026. An accented letter counts the same as a plain one. The test works with the lower figure.
- **Section 8b shows the line is safe.** It fills the app's other keys to their caps, each as long as it gets (`itapp.lasttype.v1` at 2,000 pairs and `itapp.confusions.v1` at 500, under the longest ids, and `itapp.errors.v1` at 20, at the length the app cuts each one to), with realistic theme, backup, audio, seen and reading values, and adds worst-case records under made-up ids, standing in for items not written yet, until the browser copy is just under 4 MB: 25,910 records, 7,163 of them made up. Loading keeps them all (a record whose id isn't an item yet is kept), the browser agrees to save the copy, it loads back intact after a reload, and every cloud document stays under 200 KB (the largest shard is 138.8 KB, against a limit of 256 KB). The other nine keys then take 97.8 KB and all ten together 4.09 MB, leaving **0.90 MB** of the 5 MiB spare (0.91 MB measured in Chromium 153). The test fails if less than 0.5 MB would be left.
- **Imported packs share the same space.** `itapp.packs.v1`, the packs imported by hand on Your data, counts against the same 5 MiB. Section 8b leaves it empty, since Grant doesn't import packs; anyone who imports a big one has that much less room.
- **Saving takes longer as the copy grows.** The whole browser copy is saved after every answer. With the CPU slowed 4×, about as fast as a mid-range phone, one `saveProgress()` takes about **170–180 ms** at today's worst case and about **245–275 ms** just under 4 MB, more than half of it turning progress into text (each save timed on its own, half a second after the last, the median of seven, over two runs). The test prints both figures; they are recorded, not held to a budget. A learner who has practised only part of the course saves far less.
- **Firefox and Safari haven't been measured.** Playwright's Firefox and WebKit couldn't be downloaded in the session that raised the line (the network blocked the download hosts), so the figures above are Chromium's. Measure them before relying on the line in Firefox (on the Ubuntu computer, say) or sharing the app more widely.
- **The worst case is a long way off.** It needs a record for every item, which means every grammar unit, all of the Verbs section and all ten vocab packs practised. A record is about 153 characters, much the same whether an item has been seen 3 times or 123, so the figure grows with the number of items practised, not with how long they have been practised.
- **If the browser ever refuses to save,** `saveProgress()` notices (`S.saveFailed`) and Today says "Progress couldn't be saved" with a Back up now button, so nothing is lost silently. The cloud copy (claude.ai only) is not affected: its 16 shards are about 102 KB each at today's worst case and at most about 139 KB just under the 4 MB line, against a 256 KB limit.

## Where the space goes

Each record is saved as an object, as `"fc10-laccio":{"box":7,"due":…,"lapses":12,"lastSeen":…,"lastWrong":…,"seenCount":123,"lastRight":…}`. The field names take about 41% of the browser copy, the numbers about 35%, the item ids about 10%, the daily log about 7% (0.22 MB for two years) and punctuation the rest.

## Options, from least to most work

1. **Raise the test's limit from 3 MB to 4 MB.** **Done on 3 November 2026, with v9.24 on `main`.** Nothing in the app changes; the test simply allows more of the real 5 MiB. This gives room for about 7,160 more records and, with every other key full, still leaves about 0.9 MB of the browser's 5 MiB spare (section 8b). Quick and safe, but it only moves the line.
2. **Leave out fields that are 0.** `checkRec` already accepts a record with a field missing, but `applyResult` adds to the counts directly (`r.seenCount++`), so the loader would have to put the zeros back first, and an older version would lose those counts. It saves up to about a sixth for a typical learner (many records have no lapses and no wrong answer yet) but nothing in the worst case, so it doesn't help the test.
3. **Save records as arrays, as the cloud already does** (`[box, due, lapses, lastSeen, lastWrong, seenCount, lastRight]`, via `packRec` and `unpackRec`). This takes each record from about 153 characters to about 85, bringing today's worst case to about 1.74 MB, with room for about 27,900 more records under the 4 MB line. It needs care, because an older version reading the new format would treat every record as damaged and start afresh. So the packed copy should go under a new key (for example `itapp.progress.v2`), and the new version should read the old key once if the new one is missing, then remove the old key (see the warning below); an older version opened afterwards then finds no progress of its own but leaves the packed copy alone, and a backup file restores it. Backups should stay in today's readable format, or the app must read both. Tests to update: the progress test (formats, migration, a rollback to the previous version), the speed test (saving after an answer packs every record) and the speed-ups test.

   **Warning: the move must never need both keys at once.** The two copies only just fit side by side today: a 2.95 MB old copy, a packed copy of about 1.74 MB and 0.1 MB of other keys come to 4.79 MB of the 5 MiB (5.00 MB). About 930 more items, an old copy of about 3.1 MB, and they no longer fit; near the 4 MB line they would need about 6.4 MB (4.00 MB old and about 2.32 MB packed, working from the figures above: 153 and 85 characters a record). So the move should read the old copy into memory, remove the old key, then write the packed copy, all in one go with nothing in between; if that write fails, it puts the old copy back (it fitted before) and shows Today's "Progress couldn't be saved" card, with its Back up now button. Its tests should include a 4 MB worst case, built as section 8b builds it, and a write the browser refuses.
4. **Move progress to IndexedDB.** Its limit is hundreds of megabytes, but it is asynchronous, so loading at start-up, every save and the backup code would need reworking, and the installed copy's migration would need testing on the phone. This is the natural home for progress in a native app, so it is better done as part of that move than before it.

Splitting the browser copy into several keys does not help, because the 5 MiB is shared by the whole site. Compressing the text would save the most space, but it costs time on every save and would add a library to the single file, so it isn't worth it.

## Recommendation

Option 3 comes next. Nothing needs to change for now: with the 4 MB line the course has room for about 7,160 more records, and section 8b shows the line is safe with every other key full. Do option 3 before the room under 4 MB falls below about 2,000 records, which is when the progress test's note appears ("it's time for option 3"), or sooner if saves get noticeably slow on the phone. It frees the most room, makes each save smaller and quicker, and reuses the packing the cloud copy has used since 9.4; mind its warning, since the two copies stop fitting side by side soon. If the native app comes first, its own storage replaces all of this, and the 4 MB line covers any content added in the meantime.
